What's Going Down at the 2028 LA Olympics

This isn't a one-sport show. The 2028 Games are the largest Olympics ever staged — roughly 11,000 athletes from more than 200 nations competing in 36 sports across 353 medal events over 17 days. It's the third time Los Angeles has hosted the Summer Olympics, after 1932 and 1984, and the whole city becomes the stadium.

You'll see the classics — track & field, swimming, gymnastics, basketball, soccer, volleyball, boxing, and tennis — plus skateboarding, surfing, and 3x3 basketball. And LA28 is adding five new sports you won't have seen at recent Games:

  • Flag Football — the 5-on-5, non-contact format, making its Olympic debut
  • Squash — also debuting at the Olympics for the first time ever
  • Cricket (T20) — returning to the Games with six-team men's & women's tournaments
  • Baseball & Softball — back on the program, a natural fit for LA
  • Lacrosse (Sixes) — returning in its fast, six-a-side format

Closest to LAX: the Inglewood cluster minutes from LAXCC hosts the Opening Ceremony (at SoFi Stadium), Olympic swimming (SoFi becomes the largest swim venue ever in Week 2), and basketball (next door at the Intuit Dome). Track & field and the Closing Ceremony land at the nearby Coliseum, with gymnastics and indoor sports downtown and water sports in Long Beach. Whatever you're flying in to watch, LAXCC is on the way.

When & Where Are the 2028 Olympics in Los Angeles?

Los Angeles hosts the Games of the XXXIV Olympiad from July 14 to July 30, 2028, with a handful of team sports kicking off two days early on July 12. The LA28 Paralympic Games follow from August 15 to August 27, 2028.

Instead of building a single Olympic park, LA28 spreads events across venues the city already has — from Downtown LA and Exposition Park to Long Beach, Carson, and the coast. Cannabis Rules to Know Before the Games

California legalized adult-use cannabis with Proposition 64 in 2016, so anyone 21 or older with a valid government photo ID can buy. If you're visiting from out of state or abroad, you do not need a California ID — a passport or your home state's driver's license works fine. But there are a few rules that catch visitors off guard, so plan ahead.

1. You Can't Consume in Public — or at the Venues

Smoking, vaping, or consuming cannabis in public is illegal in California, and that includes sidewalks, parks, beaches, and the areas around stadiums. The Olympic venues themselves will be cannabis-free zones — like any major ticketed event, you should not expect to bring or use cannabis inside SoFi Stadium, the Coliseum, or any other venue. Security, bag checks, and event policy all apply.

Where you can legally consume: a private residence (your own or a host's, where the owner allows it), many cannabis-friendly hotels and rentals, or a licensed consumption lounge. You cannot consume in a moving vehicle — as a passenger or a driver — and cannabis is treated like an open container on the road.

2. Consumption Lounges Exist, But They're Limited

California allows cities to license on-site consumption lounges, and a 2024 state law (AB 1775) expanded what they can offer. West Hollywood is the best-known LA-area destination for legal lounges. If your plan is to smoke a pre-roll socially during the Games, a licensed lounge or a private space is the lawful way to do it — not the street outside the stadium.

3. Don't Fly With It

Cannabis is still federally illegal, and LAX is federal-adjacent territory at the security checkpoint. Don't pack cannabis in a carry-on or checked bag for a flight home. Buy what you'll use during your trip, and consume or leave it behind before you fly. This is exactly why LAXCC's location near the airport is convenient — grab it when you land, not before you leave.
